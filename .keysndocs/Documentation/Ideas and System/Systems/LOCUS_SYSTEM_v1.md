## 1. THE FOUR FACTIONS & YOUR OUTCOMES

The four factions are the four domains of your life that the system governs. They are not equal in priority — they have a hierarchy that activates in crisis. But under normal conditions, all four must receive attention every week. No faction starves.

### The Hierarchy (Crisis Priority Order)
```
1. Health/Stability    ← If this collapses, everything collapses
2. Leverage/Money      ← Your trajectory and independence
3. Craft/Skills        ← What makes you worth hiring and worth watching
4. Expression/Explore  ← What keeps you sane and curious
```

This hierarchy is ONLY for emergencies. In normal operation, all four factions run in parallel. The hierarchy tells you what to protect last when time is tight.

## 2. THE GOAL STACK ARCHITECTURE

Every piece of work in your life maps to exactly one node in this tree. If it doesn't map to anything, it's noise. Discard it or consciously add it.

```
OUTCOMES (WHY) — 5-7 max, years-long, life-changing
    └── PROJECTS (WHAT) — finite, completable, serve an Outcome
            └── TASKS (HOW) — atomic, executable in 1 sitting, serve a Project
```

### Rules of the Goal Stack

**Outcomes:**
- Max 7. If you have more, you have none.
- Each belongs to exactly one Faction.
- Review quarterly. They should feel slightly uncomfortable — that means they're still real.
- A goal you haven't touched in 60 days is a dead node. Flag it. Kill it or recommit.

**Projects:**
- Must have: a clear completion state (you know when it's done), a parent Outcome, a Faction.

- Must NOT have vague verbs: "work on," "think about," "look into." Every task starts with a concrete verb: Write, Code, Film, Edit, Read, Call, Email, Design, Record, Practice, Build, Research.

## 3. THE COMPLETE METRIC FRAMEWORK

Everything runs on numbers. No numbers, no system.

### 3.1 PERSONAL METRICS — Logged Every Morning (2 minutes)

| Metric | Name | Range | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| `E` | Energy | 1–10 | Physical + mental readiness right now. 1 = can barely function. 10 = locked in. |
| `M` | Mood | 1–10 | Emotional baseline. 1 = very low/distressed. 10 = genuinely good. |
| `S` | Sleep | 1–10 | Last night's sleep quality. 1 = terrible, broken, exhausted. 10 = full, rested, deep. |
| `ST` | Stress | 1–10 | Background stress load. 1 = calm. 10 = overwhelmed, can't think straight. |
| `T` | Time | hours | Available hours today after all fixed commitments. Be realistic. |

**Logging rules:**
- Log these BEFORE coffee, BEFORE phone. Baseline reading only.
- If you're unsure between two numbers, pick the lower one. The system is calibrated for honesty.
- These take 90 seconds. There is no excuse to skip them.

---

### 3.2 TASK METRICS — Assigned When a Task is Created

| Metric | Name | Range | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| `P` | Priority | 1–10 | How critical is this to its parent Outcome? 1 = nice-to-have. 10 = blocking everything. |
| `D` | Difficulty | 1–10 | Cognitive + physical load. 1 = mindless. 10 = maximum focus required. |
| `U` | Urgency | 1–10 | Deadline pressure. 1 = no deadline. 10 = due today or tomorrow. |
| `Est` | Estimate | hours | Realistic time to complete this task. Not optimistic. Add 20%. |

**Task Weight Score (TWS):**
```
TWS = (P × 0.4) + (U × 0.4) + (D × 0.2)
```
Higher TWS = schedule first. This is the sorting key for your day.

**After a task is completed, log:**

| Metric | Name | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| `A` | Actual time | How long it actually took. Feeds future estimates. |
| `Q` | Quality | 1–10. How well did you do it? Be honest. This builds your own quality history. |
| `R` | Reason for deviation | If actual ≠ estimated by >50%, note why. One sentence. |

---

### 3.3 FACTION HEALTH METRICS — Calculated Weekly

| Metric | Formula | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Completion Rate (CR) | tasks_done / tasks_attempted × 100 | Are you finishing what you start? |
| Consistency Score (CS) | days_with_output / 7 × 100 | Are you showing up? |
| Momentum Score (MS) | Average CR over last 3 weeks | Are you accelerating or decelerating? |
| Lag Score (LS) | target_hours - actual_hours (this week) | How far behind target are you? |
| Action Gap (AG) | declared_priority - behavioral_priority | Are you lying to yourself about what matters? |

**Action Gap Explained:**
- You say a faction has Priority 9 in your life.
- But you've given it 2 hours this week vs. 17.5 target.
- Action Gap = 9 (declared) − 2 (actual ÷ target × 10) = 7.
- An Action Gap above 5 is a system alert. You are avoiding something. The system flags this for your weekly review.

## 4. DAILY CAPACITY SCORE — THE MASTER NUMBER

Every morning, after logging E, M, S, ST, the system computes your Daily Capacity Score.

```
DCS = ((E + M + S) / 3) × (1 − ST/20)
```

**What this produces:**

| DCS | Mode | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 – 2.0 | SURVIVAL | You are not okay. Protect the non-negotiables. That's it. |
| 2.1 – 4.0 | RECOVERY | Below baseline. Gentle movement only. No hard pushes. |
| 4.1 – 6.0 | NORMAL | Standard operating. A full, balanced day is possible. |
| 6.1 – 8.0 | DEEP WORK | You are sharp. Use this for your hardest, highest-value work. |
| 8.1 – 10.0 | PEAK | Rare. Pull out your most ambitious tasks. Do not waste this. |

**Example:**
- E=6, M=7, S=5, ST=4
- DCS = ((6+7+5)/3) × (1 − 4/20) = 6.0 × 0.8 = **4.8 → Normal Mode**

## 5. THE FIVE OPERATING MODES

The mode your DCS lands in dictates the shape of your entire day.

### MODE 0: SURVIVAL (DCS 0–2)
```
Permission granted to do nothing productive.
Non-negotiables only: eat, sleep, don't break academic deadlines.
No guilt. The system knows. It will redistribute.
```

### MODE 1: RECOVERY (DCS 2.1–4)
```
Task selection rules:
- D ≤ 4 only (nothing cognitively hard)
- Maximum 2 tasks outside non-negotiables
- Prefer Faction 4 (Expression/Explore) — easy, restorative
- No creative work that requires deep focus
- Sleep adjustment: aim to be in bed 1 hour earlier tonight
```

### MODE 2: NORMAL (DCS 4.1–6)
```
Task selection rules:
- D ≤ 7 allowed
- 3–4 tasks outside non-negotiables
- Balance across factions: at least 2 factions must get attention
- Check Lag Score: lagging faction gets priority bump of +2 to all its tasks' TWS
- Standard time blocking: deep work in morning, lighter tasks afternoon
```

### MODE 3: DEEP WORK (DCS 6.1–8)
```
Task selection rules:
- D ≤ 9 allowed
- 4–5 tasks outside non-negotiables
- Schedule highest TWS tasks in a single 2–3 hour uninterrupted block
- Protect this block: no calls, no social media, phone in another room
- Use afternoon for faction catch-up (lagging factions)
- This is when real progress happens. Treat it like a gift.
```

### MODE 4: PEAK (DCS 8.1–10)
```
Task selection rules:
- All difficulty levels available
- Pull your single most ambitious task — the one you've been avoiding
- 5–6 tasks possible
- Do the hard thing first. Don't warm up. You are already warm.
- Document what you produce today. This is your best work.
```

## 6. THE DAILY OPERATING PROCEDURE

This is the repeating cycle. Every day runs through this loop.

### MORNING PROTOCOL (10 minutes total)

**Step 1 — Physical log in journal (2 min):**
Write four numbers: E / M / S / ST
Write one sentence: "Today I want to ___"

**Step 2 — Calculate DCS (30 sec):**
Either mentally or on paper.
Determine your mode.

**Step 3 — Pull today's task list (5 min):**
From Obsidian, open your active Projects.
Select tasks using this decision tree:

```
START
│
├── What is my mode today?
│
├── [SURVIVAL] → Non-negotiables only. Done.
│
├── [RECOVERY] → 
│   ├── Lock non-negotiables
│   ├── Select 1-2 tasks where D ≤ 4
│   └── Prefer Faction 4 tasks
│
├── [NORMAL / DEEP / PEAK] →
│   ├── Lock non-negotiables (mark time blocks)
│   ├── Calculate available T after non-negotiables
│   ├── Check all Faction Lag Scores
│   │   └── If any faction LS > 3 hrs behind: +2 TWS bonus to all its tasks
│   ├── Sort remaining tasks by TWS (highest first)
│   ├── Pick top tasks that fit within T (leaving 20% buffer)
│   ├── Distribute: at least 2 different factions must appear
│   └── If two tasks have equal TWS → pick the one from the lagging faction
│
└── Result: Today's task list (max 6 items including non-negotiables)
```

**Step 4 — Block into Google Calendar (2 min):**
- Morning block (your best hours): highest D task
- Afternoon: medium D tasks
- Evening: lowest D tasks, admin, review

---

### EVENING PROTOCOL (7 minutes total)

**Step 1 — Log completions (3 min):**
For each task attempted today, record: A (actual time), Q (quality 1-10), done/deferred.

**Step 2 — Deferred task rule:**
If a task was deferred, note why. One sentence.
If the same task has been deferred 3+ times: it gets flagged as a **Deferral Pattern**. This goes to weekly review. You are avoiding something. The system wants to know why.

**Step 3 — Evening journal entry (4 min):**
Three sentences max:
- "What I actually did today:"
- "What I avoided / didn't do, and why:"
- "Tomorrow's single most important task:"

## 7. THE SCHEDULING ALGORITHM

This is the full logic for how time gets allocated. This runs daily and is recalculated weekly.

### DAILY SCHEDULING DECISION TREE

```
INPUT: DCS, T (time available), all Active Tasks with TWS scores, Faction Lag Scores

STEP 1: LOCK NON-NEGOTIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────
Non-negotiables consume time first. They are immovable.
Your non-negotiables:
  - Sleep: 7 hrs minimum (blocks everything else)
  - Movement: 20 min (can be morning or evening)
  - Meals: 3x (estimated 45 min total)
  - Academic deadlines within 48 hrs: these are treated as P=10 U=10
  - Any commitment made to another person (meeting, call, etc.)

T_available = T − non_negotiable_hours

STEP 2: APPLY DCS FILTER
────────────────────────────────────────────
Filter task pool:
  - SURVIVAL: pool = empty (no discretionary tasks)
  - RECOVERY: pool = tasks where D ≤ 4
  - NORMAL: pool = tasks where D ≤ 7
  - DEEP / PEAK: pool = all tasks

STEP 3: APPLY LAG BONUS
────────────────────────────────────────────
For each faction, calculate this week's Lag Score.
  - Faction behind by > 3 hrs: all its tasks get TWS + 2
  - Faction behind by > 6 hrs: all its tasks get TWS + 4 (this faction is in crisis)
  - Faction ahead of target: tasks get TWS − 1 (slightly deprioritize, let others catch up)

STEP 4: SORT AND SELECT
────────────────────────────────────────────
Sort filtered pool by adjusted TWS (descending).
Select tasks from the top of the sorted list, accumulating estimated time.
Stop when: accumulated Est ≥ T_available × 0.80 (keep 20% buffer for overruns)

STEP 5: FACTION BALANCE CHECK
────────────────────────────────────────────
Review selected task list.
IF all selected tasks are from ≤ 1 faction:
  → Replace the lowest TWS selected task with the highest TWS task from an underrepresented faction
  (Unless you are in SURVIVAL or RECOVERY mode)

STEP 6: TIME BLOCK ASSIGNMENT
────────────────────────────────────────────
Assign tasks to time slots by this rule:
  - Highest D task → first available deep work window (typically morning)
  - Medium D tasks → midday blocks
  - Lowest D tasks → late afternoon or evening
  - Faction 4 (Expression) tasks → evening preferred (restorative)

OUTPUT: Ordered task list with time blocks → push to Google Calendar
```

### WEEKLY REBALANCING

Every Sunday, run the weekly algorithm:

```
1. Calculate actual hours per faction this week
2. Calculate Lag Score per faction
3. Calculate Momentum Score per faction (avg CR over 3 weeks)
4. Identify Action Gap per faction
5. Flag any Deferral Patterns (tasks deferred 3+ times)
6. Plan next week's task selection priorities:
   - Faction with highest Lag Score gets first pick of your best hours next week
   - Dead nodes (goals not touched in 60 days) get flagged for review
7. Update Obsidian weekly review note with all of the above
```

## 8. THE AI LAYER — WHERE INTELLIGENCE LIVES

AI is not a chatbot in this system. AI is a processing layer that runs at specific trigger points. It does five things, and only five things.

### AI FUNCTION 1: TASK BREAKDOWN
**Trigger:** You have a new Project or a large fuzzy task.
**Input:** Project name, Outcome it serves, rough deadline.
**Output:** Atomic task list with D, P, U, Est pre-assigned.

**Prompt template:**
```
You are a task decomposition engine. Break this project into atomic tasks.
Each task must: start with a concrete verb, be completable in one sitting, have
an estimated time, a difficulty (1-10), a priority (1-10), and map to a clear
deliverable. Do not combine tasks. Err toward smaller rather than larger.

Project: [name]
Outcome it serves: [outcome]
Deadline: [date or "none"]
Known constraints: [anything I know about this]
```

---

### AI FUNCTION 2: PATTERN DETECTION
**Trigger:** Weekly review (every Sunday).
**Input:** This week's full log — DCS scores, tasks completed/deferred, faction hours, Q scores, journal entries.
**Output:** 3–5 observations about patterns, without judgment. Factual.

**What the AI looks for:**
- Which DCS scores correlate with your highest Q scores? (When are you actually best?)
- Which tasks do you consistently defer? (What are you avoiding?)
- Which faction is chronically lagging? (What are you neglecting?)
- Where is the biggest Action Gap? (Where are you lying to yourself?)
- What time of day produces your best work?

---

### AI FUNCTION 3: SCHEDULING OVERRIDE REASONING
**Trigger:** Two tasks have equal or near-equal TWS and you can't decide.
**Input:** Both tasks, current DCS, faction lag scores, last 7 days of activity.
**Output:** A recommendation with a one-sentence reasoning.

This is a tiebreaker only. The algorithm makes most decisions. AI intervenes when numbers don't differentiate.

---

### AI FUNCTION 4: CONTENT DRAFTING
**Trigger:** A project milestone is completed, a skill is demonstrated, a reflection is worth sharing.
**Input:** What was achieved, raw notes from Obsidian, the platform (LinkedIn or Instagram).
**Output:** Draft post. Not final — just a solid starting point.

Detailed in Section 14.

---

### AI FUNCTION 5: WEEKLY SELF-REPORT
**Trigger:** End of each week, after the weekly review.
**Input:** All this week's logs, journal entries (transcribed), metrics summary.
**Output:** A 3–4 paragraph written report — a mirror. Not instructions. Not a pep talk. What actually happened this week, what the data says about you, and one question worth sitting with.

## 9. THE SIGNAL FILTER — PATTERN OVER TIME

Not all data is equally valuable. A single bad day tells you nothing. Eleven bad Mondays tells you everything.

### Signal Weighting Rules

| Event | Signal Level | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Single task deferred once | Low | 0.1 |
| Same task deferred 3+ times | High | 1.0 |
| DCS below 4 once | Low | 0.1 |
| DCS below 4 for 5+ consecutive days | Critical | 1.0 → flag for wellness check |
| Faction Lag > 6 hrs once | Medium | 0.5 |
| Faction Lag > 6 hrs for 3+ weeks | High | 1.0 → system alert |
| Quality score Q ≥ 8 on specific task type | High | 1.0 → strength signal |
| Quality score Q ≤ 4 on specific task type | Medium | 0.5 → watch for pattern |
| Action Gap > 5 for a faction | High | 1.0 → confront in review |

### The Deferral Detector

A task deferred once: normal. Life happened.
A task deferred twice: note it.
A task deferred three times: **Deferral Pattern flagged.**

When a Deferral Pattern is flagged, you must answer three questions in your weekly review:
1. Is this task genuinely important? (If no: kill it.)
2. Is there a specific obstacle blocking it? (If yes: create a task to remove that obstacle first.)
3. Are you avoiding this emotionally? (If yes: why? Journal about it. Don't skip this.)

## 11. EMERGENCY & DISRUPTION PROTOCOL

Life will break your schedule. This is not a failure. The system has a response for it.

### Emergency Classification

| Type | Definition | System Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 1** | Minor disruption. Lost 1–2 hours. | Redistribute lowest-priority task to tomorrow. No recalculation needed. |
| **Level 2** | Moderate disruption. Lost a full day. | Mark today as low-DCS. Redistribute all deferred tasks. Check faction lag. |
| **Level 3** | Major disruption. Lost 3+ days (illness, family, crisis). | Enter SURVIVAL mode until resolved. When re-entering normal operation, run a fresh weekly review regardless of day. Do not try to "catch up" aggressively — this always fails. Let the Lag Score carry the information and redistribute over 2 weeks, not 1. |
| **Level 4** | Life event (exam season, project crunch, emotional crisis). | Temporarily suspend all Faction 4 discretionary work. Lock Faction 1 non-negotiables harder. Acknowledge that Faction 2 & 3 will lag. Plan the recovery window in advance. |

### The Golden Emergency Rule
```
NEVER try to recover a lost week in 3 days.
The system redistributes over 2 weeks minimum.
Aggressive catch-up creates burnout, which creates another emergency.
The Lag Score holds the memory. Trust it.
```

### New Priority Injection
When something urgent and unplanned appears:

```
1. Assign it P, D, U, Est like any other task
2. Calculate its TWS
3. If TWS > current highest TWS task in today's list:
   → Replace the lowest TWS task in today's list with the new task
   → Deferred task moves to tomorrow with U bumped +2
4. If TWS ≤ current tasks:
   → Add it to the queue normally. It will surface when it should.
5. If the new task is a true emergency (U = 10):
   → Clear the rest of today's discretionary tasks
   → Run RECOVERY mode for the remainder of the day after the emergency
```

### OBSIDIAN — The Brain

## 13. THE JOURNAL PROTOCOL

You journal sporadically. This protocol is designed for that. It is minimal enough that you can do it even on bad days. It is rich enough that it generates real signal.

### MORNING LOG (2–3 minutes)
Write these five things. Nothing else required:
```
DATE: 
E: __ / M: __ / S: __ / ST: __
DCS: __ (optional, calculate mentally)
Mode: SURVIVAL / RECOVERY / NORMAL / DEEP / PEAK
Today I want to: [one sentence, one thing]
```

### EVENING LOG (4–5 minutes)
Three sentences. Exactly three:
```
What I did today: 
What I avoided (and the honest reason):
Tomorrow's one priority:
```

### WEEKLY ENTRY (10–15 minutes, Sunday)
Free writing. No template. Questions to start if you're stuck:
- What was the real shape of this week?
- Where did I surprise myself?
- What am I still carrying that I should put down?
- What would I do differently?

### TRANSFER RULE
At minimum once a week, transcribe or summarize key insights from the journal into Obsidian `/05_JOURNAL/`. The physical book captures raw truth. Obsidian makes it searchable and AI-readable.

You do not need to transcribe everything. Transcribe the insights. Leave the noise in the book.

## 14. CONTENT & BRANDING ENGINE

Your skills, projects, and explorations should produce public output. Not everything. Not constantly. But consistently enough that LinkedIn and Instagram reflect who you actually are and what you're actually doing.

### THE TRIGGER SYSTEM

Content is not created on a schedule. Content is triggered by events:

| Trigger | Platform | Content Type |
|---|---|---|
| Project milestone reached | LinkedIn | Progress post / case study |
| New technical skill demonstrated | LinkedIn | Learning post |
| Film project completed or progressed | Both | BTS / reel / reflection |
| Open source contribution merged | LinkedIn | Technical post |
| Insight from a book or idea | Instagram | Thought post |
| Creative experiment (jewellery, pottery, etc.) | Instagram | Process / outcome photo |
| Certification completed | LinkedIn | Achievement post |
| Something made you think differently | Either | Opinion post |

### LINKEDIN CONTENT RULES
```
- Audience: recruiters, engineers, founders, collaborators
- Voice: thoughtful, competent, real — not performative
- Frequency target: 2x per week (but only when triggered, never forced)
- Length: medium (150–300 words for most posts)
- Never post: vague motivational content with no substance
- Always include: what you actually did, what you learned, what's next
```

### INSTAGRAM CONTENT RULES
```
- Audience: creatives, curious people, potential collaborators
- Voice: personal, aesthetic, exploratory
- Frequency target: 3–4x per week (can include stories)
- Content: behind the scenes, finished work, experiments, life
- Never: performative hustle content. Authentic only.
```

### AI DRAFTING WORKFLOW
When a trigger fires:
```
1. Open Obsidian → /06_CONTENT/[Platform]/
2. Create new file: YYYY-MM-DD_[topic].md
3. Write raw notes: what happened, key facts, how you feel about it
4. Run AI draft prompt:
   "Draft a [LinkedIn/Instagram] post about [topic]. 
   Here are my raw notes: [paste notes].
   My voice is: direct, curious, honest. Not polished. Not corporate.
   Audience: [recruiters and engineers / creatives and curious people].
   Length: [150-300 words / short caption].
   Do not use buzzwords. Do not start with 'I'm excited to share.'"
5. Edit the draft. Make it sound like you.
6. Post. Log it in the project file.
```

## 15. SYSTEM CALIBRATION RULES

The system is a living thing. It must be calibrated over time, or it becomes stale and you stop trusting it.

### Things You Can Change Anytime
- Task P, D, U estimates (update as you learn your own patterns)
- Faction target hour allocations (monthly review)
- Which projects are active vs. paused
- Content triggers (add new ones as your life changes)

### Things That Require a Weekly Review to Change
- Mode thresholds (the DCS cutoffs for each mode)
- TWS formula weights (P×0.4, U×0.4, D×0.2)
- Faction hierarchy in crisis scenarios

### Things That Require a Quarterly Review to Change
- The Outcomes themselves
- The Faction definitions
- The non-negotiables list

### Warning Signs the System is Failing
```
IF you are gaming your metrics (inflating E/M to feel better) → system is failing
IF you are skipping the evening log more than 3x per week → system is failing
IF the DCS hasn't influenced your task selection in a week → system is failing
IF all 4 Factions have Lag > 5 hrs → you are overwhelmed, not failing. Level 3 emergency.
IF you haven't done a weekly review in 3 weeks → system is degrading. Do one now.
```

### The Restart Protocol
If the system collapses completely — you stopped logging, stopped reviewing, lost the thread:

```
1. Do NOT try to reconstruct the past. Accept the data gap.
2. Run a fresh weekly review today, as if it's week one.
3. Set all Lag Scores to zero. Start clean.
4. Recommit to the 3 minimum daily inputs: E/M/S/ST log, task list, evening 3-sentence log.
5. The system will rebuild signal within 2 weeks.
```

> **The system forgiving you is not weakness. It is the design. A system that can't survive your impulsiveness was never built for you.**

## APPENDIX: METRIC QUICK REFERENCE

```
PERSONAL METRICS (daily morning)
  E  = Energy         1-10
  M  = Mood           1-10  
  S  = Sleep quality  1-10
  ST = Stress         1-10
  T  = Time available (hours)

MASTER FORMULA
  DCS = ((E + M + S) / 3) × (1 − ST/20)

DCS → MODE
  0.0–2.0  SURVIVAL
  2.1–4.0  RECOVERY
  4.1–6.0  NORMAL
  6.1–8.0  DEEP WORK
  8.1–10.0 PEAK

TASK METRICS (assigned at creation)
  P   = Priority   1-10
  D   = Difficulty 1-10
  U   = Urgency    1-10
  Est = Estimate   hours

TASK WEIGHT SCORE
  TWS = (P × 0.4) + (U × 0.4) + (D × 0.2)

POST-TASK LOG
  A = Actual time taken
  Q = Quality 1-10
  R = Reason for deviation (if |A − Est| > 50%)

FACTION HEALTH (weekly)
  CR = Completion Rate     = done/attempted × 100
  CS = Consistency Score   = active_days/7 × 100
  MS = Momentum Score      = avg CR over 3 weeks
  LS = Lag Score           = target_hours − actual_hours
  AG = Action Gap          = declared_priority − (actual_hrs/target_hrs × 10)

LAG BONUS FOR SCHEDULING
  LS > 3 hrs → TWS + 2 for all tasks in that faction
  LS > 6 hrs → TWS + 4 (faction in crisis)
  Faction ahead → TWS − 1
```
